97% of UK businesses have an AI skills gap — and two thirds of staff have had zero hours of formal training

The 2026 British Chambers of Commerce and Helium42 benchmark report finds AI skills gaps are near-universal. The shortage is not in technical expertise — it is in day-to-day judgement and application. And it is costing UK businesses money they cannot see.

1 NovPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 7/10Workforce signal

97% of UK organisations have at least one significant AI skills gap — this is the headline figure from the 2026 British Chambers of Commerce and Helium42 benchmark report. Two in three employees have never received a single hour of formal AI training, despite more than half of their employers actively using AI tools.

The gap is not technical — it is applied

The most important finding in this research is what kind of skill is missing. ManpowerGroup's parallel 2026 Talent Shortage Survey confirms that AI literacy and confidence in using AI within existing roles is now the most difficult skill for UK organisations to find — ahead of coding ability, data science, or machine learning expertise. The gap is in judgement: knowing when to use AI, when not to, how to evaluate the output, and how to integrate it into a real job.

This distinction matters enormously for small businesses. A service business does not need employees who can build AI models. It needs employees who can use AI tools reliably in customer-facing work, administrative tasks, and quality checks — and who know how to spot when the output is wrong before it reaches a customer.

The 2026 benchmark also estimates that £78 billion of unrealised AI value sits within the UK SME segment alone, based on scenarios where SMEs adopt AI at rates comparable to large enterprises. A separate Simply Business study found that while 70% of small business owners feel confident using AI, fewer than 48% use it regularly — the confidence-to-use gap is a skills and habit gap, not a motivation gap.

Why businesses stop at tools and skip training

The pattern is consistent: a business owner finds an AI tool that saves time, introduces it to the team, and assumes adoption follows. It rarely does at scale. Team members default to their existing workflows because they were never shown how the tool changes those workflows. The tool ends up used sporadically by one or two people rather than systematically by everyone.

Training also tends to be left to individual employees — a YouTube video here, a prompt library there — rather than structured around actual job tasks. The result is inconsistent use, patchy output quality, and a growing gap between what the business pays for AI tools and what it actually gets from them.

Operator actions — closing the gap in your business

  • Run a 30-minute team session on one AI tool per month — not a demo, a working session. Pick a real task the team does repeatedly and show how to do it with AI. Repetition with actual work tasks builds habit far faster than general AI literacy training.
  • Create a simple prompt library for your most-repeated tasks — customer reply templates, quote summaries, meeting notes, job descriptions. A shared folder with proven prompts raises the floor for everyone immediately.
  • Check what the 3% who are succeeding actually do differently — the ManpowerGroup research points to process redesign and explicit task assignment as the key differentiators. Businesses that name specific AI tasks for specific roles outperform those that say "use it when you see fit".
