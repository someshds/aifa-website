ICO secures data protection changes from Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft and four others — agentic AI now in frame

The UK Information Commissioner's Office has secured data protection improvements from ten of the world's largest AI foundation model developers. The ICO's agentic AI call for evidence closes 20 November 2026 — and the results will shape statutory requirements.

2 NovPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 8/10Governance signal

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) announced it secured data protection improvements from ten major AI foundation model developers — Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Stability AI — as it simultaneously launched a six-week call for evidence on agentic AI systems.

What the ICO secured

The ICO has not published a full list of the specific changes secured from each developer. The announcement confirms that improvements were negotiated before the ICO issued formal guidance, reflecting its stated approach of engagement before enforcement action. This is consistent with the ICO's pattern on other data protection issues — establish requirements through dialogue, then codify them into formal guidance, and finally enforce.

The agentic AI call for evidence, which runs until 20 November 2026, covers security, automated decision-making, accountability, transparency, and data minimisation in the context of AI agents — software that acts autonomously on behalf of users or organisations. The ICO has confirmed this evidence will shape its forthcoming statutory code of practice on AI and automated decision-making.

The regulatory direction

The Data Protection Act 2018 (Code of Practice on Artificial Intelligence and Automated Decision-Making) Regulations 2026 came into force on 12 May 2026, requiring the ICO to produce a statutory code of practice for AI processing involving personal data. The agentic AI call for evidence is an input to that code. Once the code is published, compliance with it will not be optional.

The Lords International Relations Committee has also announced a separate inquiry into UK AI governance, with written evidence invited by 26 November. Regulatory scrutiny of AI across Parliament, the ICO, and the CMA is running in parallel — not sequentially.

Why it matters for UK businesses

The ICO's focus is shifting from general AI training data questions (which dominated 2025) to agentic AI — systems that take actions, make decisions, and interact with personal data autonomously. If your business uses any AI tool that acts on behalf of customers, processes personal data automatically, or makes operational decisions without human review of each step, you are in scope.

The practical implication: the businesses that will respond easily to future ICO requirements are the ones with existing AI tool documentation, data flow records, and clear ownership of AI decisions. The businesses that will struggle are those that have been using AI tools informally without any governance trail.

Coverage context

ICO announcement published on ico.org.uk in late October 2026. Ten developers named explicitly: Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Stability AI. Call for evidence closes 20 November 2026. The specific changes secured from each developer have not been published. Statutory code of practice timeline has not been confirmed; ICO guidance processes typically run 12-18 months from call for evidence to final publication.

Operator actions — three things to do now

  • Start an internal AI tools register now — list every AI tool your business uses, what data it processes, and whether it makes automated decisions. One spreadsheet, reviewed quarterly, is enough to start. It is also the foundation of ICO compliance readiness.
  • Check the ICO agentic AI call for evidence — even if you don't submit, reading the questions tells you what the regulator is focused on. The six themes map directly to what future compliance requirements will look like.
  • If you use AI agents that handle customer data — review the data flow for each one before the ICO code is published. It is significantly easier to adjust a process before a formal requirement than after.
