UK Budget bets £700m on AI — what it means for your small business

The Chancellor's Autumn Budget includes a £700m AI investment package, expanded Business Rate Relief for 70,000 more firms, a tripled Trading Allowance, and the UK's first National Agentic Security Unit.

The government has put AI at the centre of the Autumn Budget. £700m committed. 70,000 more firms out of business rates. Trading Allowance tripling. And a new unit to oversee AI agents operating in UK businesses.

What the Budget includes for AI and small business

The Chancellor delivered the Autumn Budget on 29 October 2026, with AI as a central theme. The £700m AI package is designed to cement the UK's position in AI development and security, and to broaden geographic adoption — meaning government money is being directed at getting AI into regional businesses, not just London tech firms.

On business rates, the Budget expands Small Business Rate Relief to take 70,000 more companies out of paying rates altogether. This is direct cost relief for the smallest operators — if your premises currently just miss the threshold, it is worth checking again.

The Trading Allowance triples from £1,000 to £3,000, effective April 2027. For sole traders and micro-businesses using AI tools to generate additional income streams, this allowance covers more of that activity without triggering a tax reporting requirement.

EMI2 option limits double to £120,000 from April 2027, relevant for small tech companies and startups using employee share schemes to retain staff.

The National Agentic Security Unit

The Budget announced the creation of a National Agentic Security Unit — a new government body tasked with detecting and responding to AI systems operating outside their intended controls. This is the first time the UK government has created dedicated infrastructure to oversee AI agents specifically.

For businesses deploying AI agents in internal workflows, this signals that the regulatory environment around autonomous AI is tightening. The unit is not a regulator of business use — it is focused on threat response — but its existence confirms that AI agents are now treated as critical infrastructure risk by the UK government.

What the tech industry said

The UK technology sector gave a mixed response. techUK welcomed the AI investment and the British Business Bank's expanded firepower (up £5bn), but raised concerns about gaps in access to finance and tax simplification. The Sovereign AI Fund received an additional £60m in equity capacity, alongside a new Founders' Relief scheme to help startups scale without moving abroad.

Coverage context

The Autumn Budget was delivered on 29 October. The AI investment package total of £700m has been confirmed by multiple UK tech industry sources. Business rate changes require HMRC confirmation when eligibility details are published. The National Agentic Security Unit was announced in Budget documents and reported by UK tech press.

Operator actions — three things to do this week

  • Check your Business Rate Relief eligibility — with 70,000 more firms entering the exempt band, your rateable value may now fall below the threshold. Check with your local authority or accountant before the next billing period.
  • Note the April 2027 Trading Allowance change — if you use AI tools to generate any freelance or side income, the tripled allowance from April 2027 changes what you need to report.
  • Document your AI spend now — the Budget signals that AI costs will increasingly be treated as infrastructure expenditure. A clear record of what you spend on AI tools and what return you get puts you in a stronger position for any future allowances or reliefs.
