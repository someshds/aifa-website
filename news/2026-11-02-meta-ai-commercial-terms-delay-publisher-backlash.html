Meta pushes AI commercial terms to 20 November after publishers and advertisers lodge complaints with UK and EU regulators

Meta delayed its revised advertising terms — originally effective 30 October — to 20 November 2026 after industry bodies filed antitrust complaints arguing the wording gives Meta an unlimited right to use advertiser and publisher content for AI development.

2 NovPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 7/10Regulation signal

Meta has delayed the effective date of its revised commercial and self-serve advertising terms from 30 October to 20 November 2026, after industry groups filed complaints with antitrust authorities in the EU and the UK arguing that the proposed changes would hand Meta an unlimited right to use their content for AI services.

What changed in the terms

The original revised terms included wording allowing Meta to "train" AI using content from advertisers and publishers. After objections, Meta removed the word "train" from the text. However, the revised version still states that content will be used to "develop, improve, research, and maintain advertising and business products." Industry groups argue this is functionally equivalent to training permission.

A second objection concerns robots.txt. Publishers and advertisers argued that blocking Meta's crawlers via robots.txt is impractical for businesses that depend on Meta's ads crawler to run their advertising campaigns — effectively making the opt-out path unusable for most advertisers.

Who has complained

Complaints have been lodged with antitrust authorities in the EU and the UK — including the ICO and the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA). The complaints argue that the revised terms, as written, grant Meta rights to use content for AI development without meaningful consent or an effective mechanism to refuse.

Why it matters for UK businesses

If your business runs Facebook or Instagram advertising, your ad creative, audience data, and targeting information may be used to develop Meta's AI products under the revised terms when they take effect on 20 November. This applies to Meta Business Suite advertisers, small business ad accounts, and publishers with Facebook integrations.

This is separate from the question of whether Meta's AI Llama models are trained on your content. It is about the advertising data and creative you upload to Meta's ad platforms.

Coverage context

Delay confirmed by multiple sources including Unite.ai, PPC Land, and Storyboard18. Original effective date was 30 October 2026. New date is 20 November 2026. UK regulatory bodies involved: ICO and CMA. Meta has not confirmed whether the terms will be further amended before the November 20 date.

Operator actions — three things to do now

  • Read the updated Meta commercial terms when published on or before 20 November — check specifically for data rights language around AI development. If you run a Meta Business account, you will be bound by these terms.
  • Consider what ad creative and audience data you have shared with Meta — this includes custom audiences, uploaded customer lists, and ad creatives stored in the Meta Ads Library. Understand what data you are sharing before the terms take effect.
  • If you have concerns about the data rights language — the ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) and CMA accept representations from UK businesses. The ICO website has published guidance on AI and personal data that is relevant here.
