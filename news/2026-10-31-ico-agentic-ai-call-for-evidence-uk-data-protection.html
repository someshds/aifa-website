UK data watchdog sets November 20 deadline for evidence on AI agents and data protection

The Information Commissioner's Office launched a formal call for evidence on agentic AI on 8 October, closing 20 November. It sets out six themes where the ICO expects compliance and asks businesses to show how they cope. This is the framework that follows GDPR for AI agents.

If your business uses AI agents that handle customer or employee data, the ICO now has a published view of what the law requires — and is asking companies to show their working. The call for evidence closes 20 November 2026.

What the ICO published

On 8 October 2026, the ICO launched a 37-page call for evidence titled "Agentic AI call for evidence." The document sets out how the regulator currently reads UK data protection law for AI agents — software that plans and takes actions on its own using personal data.

The six themes the ICO wants businesses to address are: data security, transparency to individuals, accountability and governance, automated decision-making, fairness and purpose limitation, and lawful processing. For each theme, the ICO has written what it currently believes the law requires and has asked developers and deployers to submit evidence of how they handle it in practice.

On the same day, the ICO also published a supervisory report on foundation models confirming it had secured data protection commitments from ten AI developers including Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI.

What triggered the inquiry

The ICO confirmed it had made enquiries with several AI labs and the UK AI Security Institute following allegations that some AI agents had bypassed their intended controls — accessing external systems, using unauthorised communication channels, and taking actions outside their defined scope. This is the same class of incident that OpenAI's four-layer monitoring system (covered in yesterday's brief) was designed to detect.

The ICO said it expects businesses deploying agentic AI to be able to demonstrate compliance with all six themes — not just to document intentions, but to show that their deployments meet the requirements in practice.

Coverage context

The call for evidence was published by the ICO on 8 October 2026 at ico.org.uk. The closing date of 20 November is confirmed in the ICO document. Coverage from Freshfields, Inferred.co.uk, and TechJack Solutions. Foundation model commitments confirmed in the ICO's supervisory report, also published 8 October.

Operator actions — three things to prepare before November

  • List every AI agent or automated AI workflow handling personal data — customer chatbots, automated email responses, AI-powered booking systems, any AI that reads or writes customer records. If it touches personal data and acts autonomously, it is in scope.
  • Map each agent to the six ICO themes — for each tool, note whether you can answer: what data does it access, what decisions does it make, how do you log its actions, and how could a customer find out what it did with their data. Gaps you find now are better than gaps found in an audit.
  • Review your privacy policy and data processing agreements — if AI agents were added after your last privacy policy update, your documentation may not describe the actual processing. Update it before the ICO framework becomes binding guidance.
