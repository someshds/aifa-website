Lords defeat government 202-155 — AISI must now review powers to audit frontier AI before deployment

On 26 October, the House of Lords inserted a mandatory review of the AI Security Institute's enforcement powers into the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill — overruling the government's preference for voluntary AI developer access.

The House of Lords voted 202 to 155 on 26 October to defeat Amendment 163 — requiring the government to conduct a formal review of whether the AI Security Institute should be given statutory powers to audit frontier AI systems before they are deployed in the UK.

🔊Listen to this article

What the vote does

The amendment writes into the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill a mandatory review — within one year of the Act passing — of whether the AI Security Institute should hold powers to require pre-deployment audits of frontier AI models. It does not immediately grant AISI those powers; it requires the government to assess and publish a formal position on them within a defined timeframe.

The government had argued against the amendment on the grounds that a regulatory role for AISI would undermine its voluntary access partnerships with leading AI labs — access that allows the institute to examine models before release. The Lords' counter-argument, which won the vote, was that voluntary arrangements are fragile: the US government suspended AISI's access for three weeks earlier in 2026 without any regulation triggering or preventing that withdrawal.

The AISI access problem this vote highlights

AISI's current model relies on AI developers agreeing to share access. That access was suspended for three weeks by the US government in early 2026, demonstrating that the voluntary arrangement can be withdrawn at any time by a foreign government without legal recourse from the UK. The Lords' vote reflects a growing view that trust-based access is not a durable foundation for a body tasked with national AI security oversight.

The government also used the same bill to give the Secretary of State new powers (Amendment 70) to regulate AI agents affecting critical national infrastructure — but those powers are reactive, operating after incidents rather than before them. The Lords inserted the AISI review requirement precisely because the bill's existing powers only kick in post-incident.

Operator actions — what this means for UK businesses

  • Track the AISI review timeline — once the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill receives Royal Assent, the one-year clock starts. The review outcome will determine whether frontier AI providers face formal UK pre-deployment requirements. That affects what AI tools are available here and on what terms.
  • Note that the Lords also opened an AI governance inquiry — if you work in sectors that interact with government or regulated industries, submitting evidence to the Lords International Relations Committee inquiry (deadline 26 November) is one way to ensure SME operator concerns are on record.
  • Document the AI tools your business uses — when pre-deployment audit requirements or reporting frameworks arrive, businesses that already maintain a clear internal register of AI tools will spend far less time responding than those starting from scratch.
