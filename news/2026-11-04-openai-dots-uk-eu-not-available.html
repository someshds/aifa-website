OpenAI Dots: always-on AI agents with 4,000+ app connections — but not available in the UK, EEA, or Switzerland at launch

OpenAI launched Dots at DevDay on 29 September: persistent AI agents, each with their own cloud computer and browser, connecting to over 4,000 apps. Sam Altman cited "strict regulations" for the UK and EEA exclusion. Timeline for UK access: one to two quarters. What this means for UK operators now.

OpenAI Dots — always-on AI agents with their own cloud computers and 4,000+ connected apps — are live on the ChatGPT Pro plan in most of the world. They are not available in the UK, the European Economic Area, or Switzerland. Sam Altman attributed the exclusion to "strict regulations" in those regions. Business Premium users on ChatGPT can access Dots in supported regions, but UK-based Pro subscribers cannot.

What OpenAI Dots actually does

Dots are persistent AI agents inside ChatGPT. Unlike the standard ChatGPT conversation, a Dot has its own cloud computer with a browser, can run in the background while you work on something else, and maintains memory across sessions. It connects to over 4,000 apps — covering communication, finance, e-commerce, documents, and project management tools commonly used by small businesses.

The concept is closer to a virtual employee than a chatbot: set a Dot to monitor your inbox for a specific type of enquiry, check your Shopify orders every morning and flag anomalies, or watch a competitor's pricing page and alert you to changes. These are tasks that previously required either a developer or a subscription to a specialised automation platform.

Dots launched at OpenAI's DevDay on 29 September 2026 on the Pro plan at $100 per month (approximately £80). OpenAI said at launch that Business Premium users would also gain access in supported regions as part of the product rollout.

Why the UK and EEA are excluded

The exclusion is regulatory compliance, not a technical limitation. Dots collects and processes personal data from connected accounts — email, calendar, banking, communication — in ways that require careful alignment with both the UK GDPR and the EU AI Act's provisions on automated decision-making and high-risk AI systems.

Sam Altman's characterisation of this as "strict regulations" is diplomatically vague. The more precise issue is that the product connects to financial and health-related apps (the 4,000-app partner list includes banking tools, HR platforms, and medical scheduling systems), and those connection types carry additional compliance requirements in the UK and EEA that OpenAI has not yet satisfied. The timeline for compliance documentation is estimated at one to two quarters — meaning access is most likely in Q1 or Q2 2027.

The ICO's current agentic AI call for evidence (closing 20 November) is precisely the regulatory framework that products like Dots need to navigate before UK launch. OpenAI is likely tracking this process closely.

What it means for UK operators right now

If you are on ChatGPT Pro and saw the Dots announcement, your UK account will not show the feature. This is not a bug. The absence is intentional and regulatory in origin.

The more important strategic point: UK operators should not wait for Dots before building AI-assisted workflows. The same categories of work that Dots addresses — inbox monitoring, data aggregation across apps, automated reporting — are achievable today using tools that do not have an access restriction. Microsoft Copilot Actions (now available to UK M365 Business subscribers), Claude API-based custom tools, and n8n or Make.com automation workflows can achieve similar results without the pending compliance gap.

  • If you are on ChatGPT Pro expecting Dots — it is not currently available in the UK. No action needed; check back in Q1 2027.
