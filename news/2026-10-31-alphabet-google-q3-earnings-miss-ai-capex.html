Google's parent drops 13% — what the AI capex reckoning means for your tools

Alphabet reported its worst trading day since its 2004 IPO after Q3 2026 earnings showed negative free cash flow and AI infrastructure spend outpacing revenue growth. The combined big-four AI capex bill for 2026 now exceeds £580 billion.

31 OctPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 8/10Market signal

Alphabet's share price fell 13% on 30 October — its steepest single-day drop since its stock market listing — after Q3 2026 earnings showed the company burning through more cash on AI infrastructure than it generates in operating income.

🔊Listen to this article

What the numbers showed

Alphabet's Q3 2026 earnings per share came in at $2.67 against an expected $3.02. Capital expenditure in the quarter was $51.36 billion — covering data centres, AI chips, and infrastructure buildout — against operating cash flow of $42.29 billion. The gap meant the company reported negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion for the quarter.

Management held its full-year 2026 capex guidance at $195–$205 billion and signalled a further "significant increase" in 2027. Investors did not react well: £500 billion in market value was erased in a single trading session.

Alphabet is not alone. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft together spent more than $740 billion on capital expenditure across 2026. The combined bill now exceeds the GDP of Poland.

The pattern for UK businesses to watch

When the companies building the AI platforms your tools run on are spending faster than they earn, there are two likely outcomes: price increases to cover costs, or a pullback in feature development for lower-margin users. Neither is catastrophic, but both are worth anticipating.

Google Cloud — which includes the Workspace products used by many UK SMEs — reported stronger-than-expected revenue in the quarter. But the overall earnings miss dominated investor reaction and signals that the AI infrastructure investment cycle is under genuine financial strain.

The counterpoint is that this infrastructure spending is building capability: better models, more compute, faster response times. The question for a small business is whether the cost of that capability will eventually land with you.

Coverage context

Q3 2026 earnings were reported on 28–30 October. The 13% share price drop and negative free cash flow figures are confirmed by CNBC, Seeking Alpha, and Investing.com. Capex aggregates across Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft are from TechCrunch analysis. Google Cloud revenue figures are from the Alphabet earnings release.

Operator actions — three things to watch

  • Review your AI tool subscriptions before year-end — if pricing changes are coming to Google Workspace or other cloud AI tools, Q1 2027 is the most likely window. Lock in annual plans now where they offer a meaningful saving.
  • Note whether your tools are cloud-dependent — if you rely on a single platform's API (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic), this is a good moment to identify a fallback or alternative.
  • Watch for platform announcements in November and December — big tech companies historically announce pricing changes at the end of Q4 ahead of new-year billing cycles.
