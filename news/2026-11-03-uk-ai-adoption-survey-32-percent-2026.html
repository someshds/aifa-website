UK business AI use doubles to 32% in one year — small businesses at 34%, micro-businesses at 27%

The government's DSIT AI Adoption Survey 2026 shows AI use has doubled from 16% to 32% in a single year. Administration, marketing, and IT lead. But only 12% of AI-using businesses report higher revenue — the adoption gap is real and measurable.

3 NovPublished 2026

Impact 8/10UK adoption signal

The UK government's Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT) published its AI Adoption Survey 2026 this week, reporting that 32% of UK businesses with at least five employees used at least one AI technology in 2026 — double the 16% recorded in the same survey a year ago. The survey rests on 3,503 telephone interviews conducted January to April 2026.

Who is using AI

Adoption rises with business size. Micro-businesses (5–9 employees) are at 27%. Small businesses (10–49 employees) are at 34%. Medium businesses (50–249 employees) are at 47%. Large businesses (250+ employees) are at 52%. The gaps are real but smaller than most commentary implies — smaller UK businesses are not being left behind at the pace often assumed.

The most common areas of deployment are administration (81% of those using or planning to use AI), marketing (71%), and IT functions (66%). The driver cited most often is improving efficiency and productivity — 71% of adopters listed this as the main reason. Gaining competitive advantage was cited by 25%.

The productivity story — headline gains, uneven depth

75% of AI-using businesses report some productivity benefit. Improvements in workforce productivity were the most commonly cited outcome (71%), followed by more efficient or flexible processes (60%). So far, the headline looks strong.

The harder numbers: only half of adopters describe productivity gains as more than modest. Only 12% report higher revenue as a result of AI adoption. 95% report no change in workforce size. The survey authors note that reported benefits are generally described as modest, even where they are present.

What the gap means

The gap between adoption rate (32%) and revenue impact (12%) is not unusual for a technology in early-majority deployment. Businesses are adding AI to existing processes rather than redesigning processes around AI. That produces efficiency gains — which the survey confirms — but it rarely produces revenue growth. Revenue growth tends to come from AI enabling things that weren't possible before: faster response times that win more quotes, automation that allows the business to serve more customers without hiring, or AI-generated insights that identify new revenue opportunities.

The survey identifies administration as the most common use case. That is consistent with the efficiency-gain, revenue-neutral outcome. The businesses likely to report revenue impact in the next iteration of this survey will be the ones that moved AI into customer-facing processes — lead follow-up, quote generation, customer onboarding — in 2026.

Coverage context

Survey commissioned by DSIT, conducted Jan–Apr 2026 by Ipsos Mori. 3,503 telephone interviews plus 50 follow-up conversations and seven site visits. Published November 2026. Respondents: UK private sector businesses with at least five employees. The 16% baseline is from the equivalent 2025 survey using the same methodology. Micro-business (5–9 employees), small (10–49), medium (50–249), large (250+).

Operator actions — three things to do now

  • If you are in the 68% not yet using AI — administration is the lowest-risk entry point confirmed by this survey. Start with one admin task: meeting notes, invoice drafting, email triage. Pick a tool with a free tier and a one-week test window.
  • If you are in the 20% using AI without revenue impact — map which AI tools connect to your customer-facing revenue processes. If none do, that is the gap. Pick one: quote response time, lead follow-up speed, or proposal quality.
  • If you want to appear in the 12% revenue-impact group in 2027 — the businesses with the best chance are those rebuilding one complete process around AI rather than adding AI features to an unchanged process.
