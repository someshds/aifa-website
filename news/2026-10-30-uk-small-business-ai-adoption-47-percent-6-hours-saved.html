47% of UK small businesses use AI — and saving 6 hours per worker per week

New research from Simply Business and EY paints the clearest picture yet of where UK small businesses stand with AI — and reveals a significant gap between the businesses getting a return and those that cannot measure it.

30 OctPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 8/10News signal

AI adoption among UK small businesses has more than doubled in 12 months. The average user is now saving the equivalent of nearly a working day per person, per week. The problem: the majority cannot tell you what they are paying for it or whether it is worth it.

The adoption numbers

Insurer Simply Business published research this week finding that 47% of UK small business owners are now using AI tools — up from 22% in 2025. An additional 13% plan to start using AI tools in the next six to twelve months. That means AI adoption among UK SMEs could exceed 60% within the next year.

The most common uses reported: creating content (written copy, marketing materials), problem solving, generating ideas, and saving time on administrative tasks.

The productivity data

EY's annual CEO survey found more granular results: the median UK worker is now saving 6.4 hours per week with AI tools, up from 3.9 hours in 2025. That is a 64% increase in time saved in twelve months. For a business of five people, 6.4 hours per person per week is 32 hours per week reclaimed — roughly the equivalent of a part-time employee.

The leading benefit cited by UK CEOs: greater output without increased headcount or additional capital expenditure (34%), ahead of faster delivery (19%) and more time for higher-value work (15%).

Half of UK CEOs — 50% — named AI tools as among their organisation's top three drivers of productivity growth over the past 12 months.

The gap that matters: 86% cannot measure it

Despite these gains, only 14% of UK CEOs said they have comprehensive visibility of their AI costs and return on investment. That means 86% of the businesses reporting time savings cannot tell you how much they are spending on those tools, or whether the saving outweighs the cost.

This is not a minor oversight — it is the difference between AI being a confirmed business investment and a collection of subscriptions that may or may not be worth their combined monthly fee.

Coverage context

Simply Business survey published October 2026; sample size and methodology not confirmed at time of writing — check the primary source for statistical significance. EY CEO survey covers a broader corporate audience than SME-only, so the 6.4 hours figure reflects a mix of company sizes. Both figures directionally consistent with British Chambers of Commerce 54% adoption data from June 2026.

The three-part audit your business should run this quarter

  • List every AI tool subscription — include ChatGPT, Copilot, Grammarly, Canva AI, Jasper, any CRM with AI features. Total the monthly cost.
  • For each tool, write one sentence describing what it replaces or makes faster, and an honest estimate of time saved per week.
  • Identify the one tool with the clearest return and the one you could cancel today without missing it. That gap is where your AI budget should rebalance.

This does not need a consultant or a sophisticated system. A spreadsheet with three columns — tool, cost, weekly time saved — is enough to go from 86% visibility to a clear picture of what your AI investment is actually returning.
