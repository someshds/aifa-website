Drax, Brookfield and Radiant announce £20bn North Yorkshire AI data centre — the UK's largest grid connection becomes AI infrastructure

Drax Power Station's 4 gigawatt grid connection — the single largest in the UK — becomes the site of a potential £20 billion AI data centre development. Public consultation begins this month. Commercial customers targeted from early 2028.

3 NovPublished 2026
4 minDaily brief
Impact 7/10Infrastructure signal

Drax, Brookfield Asset Management, and Radiant announced on 3 November 2026 a joint development agreement to build more than a gigawatt of AI data centre capacity at the Drax Power Station site in North Yorkshire. Brookfield estimates total investment could exceed £20 billion if the project is built in full, with phase one targeting approximately £2.5 billion. Drax shares rose 8% on the announcement.

Why Drax Power Station

The single biggest bottleneck for new AI data centres in Europe is not capital — it is power grid access. Drax Power Station in North Yorkshire already has a 4 gigawatt grid connection and 2.6 gigawatts of dispatchable generation on site. That combination — the largest single grid connection in the UK, paired with on-site generation — makes Drax's site one of the most viable AI infrastructure locations in Europe without requiring new grid construction.

Brookfield, one of the world's largest infrastructure investors, supplies the capital through a joint venture that includes Qai — Qatar's AI company and a subsidiary of the Qatar Investment Authority. Radiant brings technical expertise in data centre development. The partnership structure reflects what AI infrastructure investment looks like at scale: sovereign wealth funds, global asset managers, and technical operators, co-investing in long-duration physical assets.

Timeline and planning

Public consultation on the planning application is expected to begin in November 2026. Phase one targets commercial customers from early 2028. The full £20 billion figure represents a build-out over multiple phases — it is a ceiling estimate for a complete development, not a committed spend for phase one.

The project will require planning approval, and large-scale data centre developments in the UK have faced scrutiny on water use, visual impact, and energy consumption. That said, the existing industrial footprint at Drax, combined with the UK government's stated priority for AI infrastructure investment, makes planning a manageable rather than blocking risk.

Why it matters for UK small businesses

The direct impact on small businesses is indirect and medium-term. More UK-based AI infrastructure means three things over the next two to three years: better data sovereignty options for businesses that need to keep data in the UK (healthcare, legal, financial services, education); increased competition in AI cloud pricing as UK infrastructure capacity grows; and stronger signal that the UK AI ecosystem has the physical backbone to support sustained growth rather than relying entirely on US hyperscaler capacity.

The Autumn Budget 2026 AI package and the Drax announcement together are consistent signals: the UK government and major private capital are both making large, long-duration bets on AI infrastructure. That is the kind of foundation that tends to attract the AI product development and talent that eventually reaches small business tooling.
