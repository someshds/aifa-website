One robots.txt line now blocks all four major AI crawlers from training on your content

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic agreed on 30 October to honour a shared opt-out signal. A single entry — User-agent: __usage_ai-training / Disallow: / — tells all four not to use your site for AI training data.

For the first time, the four largest AI labs have agreed to share a single opt-out standard. Adding one group to your website's robots.txt file tells OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic not to use your content for training their generative AI models.

What the labs agreed

On 30 October 2026, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic announced joint support for a new robots.txt group called __usage_ai-training. The token is being standardised through the IETF AI Preferences working group and defines a single, interoperable way to express a preference about AI training data collection.

To use it, a website owner adds this to their robots.txt file:

User-agent: __usage_ai-training
Disallow: /

Anthropic has already started implementing support. OpenAI says it will do so "in the coming months." Microsoft has committed to a date of 1 February 2027. Google has said "2027."

What it does and does not do

The opt-out applies to content retrieved while the robots.txt file is in place. It does not remove material that has already been collected and is present in current training sets or deployed models. If your content has already been scraped, this does not reverse that.

The standard also does not carry enforcement mechanisms. Compliance depends on each company honouring their stated commitment. The four companies have commercial and reputational incentives to comply, but there is no legal penalty for ignoring the signal.

Existing per-company robots.txt rules remain in place alongside the new shared token. If you have already blocked OpenAI's GPTBot user-agent separately, that rule still applies. The new token is additive.

Coverage context

The joint announcement was made on 30 October 2026 and reported by SiliconANGLE, DEV Community, and FourWeekMBA. The IETF internet-draft draft-car-ai-txt-wellknown-00 is the underlying standards document. Implementation timelines are from each company's individual statements.

Operator actions — three things to do

  • Check your current robots.txt — if you do not already have AI-training opt-outs in place, adding the new shared token now establishes your preference before more crawlers implement support.
  • Ask your web developer or hosting provider — the robots.txt file is usually in the root of your website. Most site platforms (Wix, WordPress, Squarespace) allow you to edit it. Your developer or support team can add the entry in under five minutes.
  • Do not expect retroactive removal — this controls future crawling only. If you have content concerns about existing training sets, that is a separate legal conversation about copyright and data protection.
