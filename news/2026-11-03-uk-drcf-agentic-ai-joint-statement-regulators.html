UK's four digital regulators publish joint agentic AI framework — existing law applies now, coordination is active

The DRCF joint statement from 30 October 2026 confirmed that the CMA, FCA, ICO and Ofcom are actively coordinating on agentic AI regulation. There is no new UK AI Act — but that does not mean there is no regulatory framework. There already is one.

Impact 8/10Governance signal

On 30 October 2026, the UK's Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum published a joint statement on protecting people and businesses in an agentic AI economy. The statement is from all four members of the DRCF — the CMA (Competition and Markets Authority), the FCA (Financial Conduct Authority), the ICO (Information Commissioner's Office), and Ofcom. It sets out how existing law already applies to AI agents and confirms that the four regulators are actively coordinating rather than working in parallel.

What the statement says

The DRCF statement covers three areas: existing legal frameworks that apply to AI agents right now; how the four regulators will continue to collaborate and work with the government as personal AI agents become more common; and how those frameworks apply to the specific capabilities and risks of agentic AI — software that takes autonomous actions, makes decisions, and interacts with personal data on behalf of users and organisations.

The statement does not create new law. It clarifies that existing law is sufficient to regulate most current agentic AI activity and signals how enforcement will work. The CMA is focused on competition effects — market concentration, default settings, lock-in. The FCA is focused on AI in financial advice, credit decisions, insurance, and investment management. The ICO is focused on data protection in agentic systems processing personal data. Ofcom is focused on AI in content, communications, and algorithmic recommendation.

Why this matters more than a new AI Act would

There is no UK AI Act and the DRCF statement confirms the UK's direction: not a single new law, but coordinated application of existing regulatory regimes. For businesses, this means the question is not "is there an AI law I need to comply with?" — the question is "which existing regulator's rules apply to how I use AI in my business?" The answer is almost always "more than one."

The ICO's agentic AI call for evidence, running until 20 November 2026, feeds directly into a forthcoming statutory code of practice on AI processing of personal data. Once that code is published, compliance with it will be mandatory. The DRCF statement and the ICO call for evidence are the precursor to that code — businesses that read these now know what's coming.

What agentic AI actually means in regulation

Agentic AI is any software system that acts autonomously on behalf of a user or organisation — booking appointments, processing documents, responding to customers, making routing decisions, managing workflows without human review of each step. Under the DRCF statement, this covers a wide range of tools already in use: AI email assistants that draft and send responses, AI scheduling tools that book meetings without approval, AI customer service systems that handle enquiries end-to-end, and AI document processing systems that extract and route data automatically.

The regulatory test is not whether a tool is labelled "AI" — it is whether it processes personal data, makes automated decisions, or affects a consumer's interests autonomously. Most AI tools deployed for productivity or customer service in a UK business meet at least one of these criteria.

Coverage context

DRCF joint statement published 30 October 2026. Covers CMA, FCA, ICO, and Ofcom. Statement available at gov.uk and individual regulator websites. ICO agentic AI call for evidence open until 20 November 2026. The statutory AI code of practice timeline has not been confirmed; ICO guidance processes typically run 12–18 months from call for evidence to final publication. Insurance-Edge.net coverage confirms the statement was published 30 October 2026.

Operator actions

  • Identify your agentic AI exposure: List every AI tool in your business that takes autonomous action or processes personal data. Email assistants, scheduling tools, customer service bots, document processors, and workflow automation tools all qualify. This is not a compliance burden — it is an asset register.
  • Assign a regulator to each tool: For each tool, identify which regulator is most relevant. Tools handling customer personal data → ICO. Tools in financial services → FCA. Tools affecting competition or consumer choice → CMA. Tools in media, content, or communications → Ofcom.
  • Read the ICO call for evidence questions: Even if you do not submit a response, reading the questions tells you the six areas the ICO will focus statutory requirements on. Those six areas are the future compliance checklist for agentic AI in UK businesses.
