# AI Fusion canonical website messaging standard

Version 1.0 — 3 October 2026

## Purpose

This standard translates the Transforming Your Work webinar into the canonical messaging system for AI Fusion websites. It governs positioning, page hierarchy, claims, calls to action and future copy reviews.

## Core position

AI is not another software category. It is a new layer of intelligence across the organisation.

AI Fusion helps a business decide what capability it needs, identify the highest-value place to begin, and implement the right combination of workflows, systems, data, tools, training and governance.

The governing principle is:

> Capability first. Tool second. Always.

## Five-second comprehension

The opening screen of a commercial page should make three things clear:

1. AI Fusion works on useful business capabilities, not disconnected AI tools.
2. Work starts by assessing the business, its workflows and its readiness.
3. The next step is a practical conversation to find the safest, highest-value starting point.

## Narrative order

Use this sequence unless the page has a narrower job:

1. Name the business problem or desired capability.
2. Explain why buying another tool is not a strategy.
3. Show the relevant workflow, system, data and people implications.
4. Present the disciplined implementation path.
5. Address adoption, governance, risk and value.
6. Invite the reader to identify one useful next step.

## The seven levels of AI value

Use this framework when explaining what AI can contribute:

1. Productivity
2. Creative empowerment
3. Personalisation
4. Automation
5. Business intelligence
6. Prediction
7. Agentic scale

Do not imply that every business should begin at the highest level. The right level depends on the business need, readiness, risk and likely value.

## The implementation method

Use these seven steps in this order:

1. Assess
2. Strategise
3. Prioritise
4. Design
5. Implement
6. Train
7. Scale

Prioritisation considers ROI, speed to implement, strategic value, team readiness, risk and scalability.

## The organisational stack

AI implementation may touch strategy, workflows, data, systems, tools, training, agents and governance. Copy should make this wider system visible whenever a page could otherwise sound tool-first.

## Agent framing

When describing agents, use three practical families:

- Business intelligence agents
- Operations agents
- Market outreach agents

Agents are designed for defined roles with permissions, oversight and escalation. They do not replace leadership, judgement, relationships or accountability.

## People, safety and governance

People are part of implementation, not an afterthought. Explain training, reassurance and adoption in plain language.

Safety claims must be specific and proportionate. Refer to deliberate access controls, permissions, private knowledge sources, approved tools, data handling, testing, monitoring and human escalation. Never make blanket security or compliance guarantees.

## Value and commercial claims

Build the business case against current inefficiency. Useful measures include hours saved, errors reduced, revenue created, speed gained and capacity unlocked.

Do not promise guaranteed ROI, fixed savings, instant transformation or a universal implementation time. Timelines and outcomes follow assessment and depend on scope, systems, data, risk and team readiness.

## Voice and style

- Use the display name **AI Fusion**.
- Use **Grant De Swardt**.
- Be direct, practical, calm and specific.
- Prefer evidence and defined outcomes to hype.
- Write for a business owner who needs clarity, not technical theatre.
- Explain unfamiliar terms at first use.
- Keep calls to action useful and low-pressure.

## Canonical call to action

The strategy call should promise to identify the single highest-value place AI or automation could help first, map the safest sensible next step, and leave the prospect with something actionable whether or not they work with AI Fusion.

## Prohibited or restricted messaging

Avoid:

- “autopilot”, “runs itself”, “while you sleep” or “without lifting a finger”
- “plug-and-play” for organisational change
- “complete AI team” or language that erases human accountability
- unsupported ROI, savings, response-time or delivery-time guarantees
- claims that a tool choice should come before diagnosis
- claims that people need no involvement or training
- blanket privacy, security or compliance guarantees
- fear-led claims that AI will simply replace the team

## Intentional exceptions

- First-party customer quotations remain faithful to the reviewer’s words.
- Editorial news and blog archives retain factual or attributed language needed to report the source material.
- Legal names, email addresses, domains, repository names and technical identifiers are not renamed solely for display-brand consistency.
- Product pages may describe a specific tool, but must place it within a defined business capability, workflow and oversight model.

## Review checklist

Before publishing, confirm that the page:

- states the business capability before naming tools;
- uses the relevant seven-level, seven-step or organisational-stack language accurately;
- includes people, training, safety and governance where material;
- makes no unsupported guarantee;
- uses AI Fusion and Grant De Swardt consistently;
- has one clear primary conversion path;
- preserves factual, legal, customer and editorial integrity.
