Aragon AI Headshots: A Practical Look

What the tool is for, how the process typically works, and why likeness and consent matter more than the render.

By AI Fusion February 12, 2024 3 min read

AI headshot tools promise studio-style portraits from photos you already have. Aragon AI is one of the better-known options for LinkedIn and team pages. This is a practical look at what the product does, how the process typically works, and what to check before you upload staff photos.

What Aragon AI Is For

Aragon generates corporate-style portraits from a set of personal photos. The pitch is straightforward: skip the studio booking, get a batch of outfit and background variations, and pick the ones that look like you. For a small team that needs consistent profile pictures, that can be useful. It is not a substitute for a photographer when likeness, lighting or brand control really matter.

How the Process Typically Works

  1. Create an account and start a new headshot project.
  2. Follow the upload guidance. Providers usually ask for ten to fourteen or more photos, with varied expressions, angles and backgrounds — not a burst of identical selfies.
  3. Submit the set and wait for generation. Turnaround is often measured in hours rather than seconds.
  4. Review the results. Expect a mix: some usable, some off in small ways (hands, hair, expression), and some that do not look like the person.

Those steps are the common pattern for this class of tool, including Aragon AI. Exact prompts, pack sizes and timings change, so treat the product's own checkout screen as the source of current details.

What to Check Before You Use the Outputs

Likeness first. If a portrait would not be recognised by a client who has met you, do not publish it. Consistency next: a leadership page where every face has a slightly different jawline or hairline looks worse than a simple, honest photo.

Then privacy. You are sending identifiable photos of people — sometimes employees — to a third-party system. That is personal data. Get consent, say where the photos will be stored, and do not upload images of anyone who has not agreed. If you would not email those files to a vendor without a conversation, do not drag-and-drop them into a generator.

Is It Worth It?

For an individual who needs a cleaner LinkedIn photo and understands the review step, a tool in this category can be a reasonable shortcut. For a company roll-out, treat it as a production process: consent, a human review, and a rule that odd or inaccurate images are discarded.

If you are choosing AI tools more broadly, start from the capability you need rather than from a single app.

The Bottom Line

AI headshots are a convenience product. The useful ones are the ones a person has checked. The risk is not the technology — it is publishing a face you did not really approve, or handing over staff photos without a clear data conversation.
